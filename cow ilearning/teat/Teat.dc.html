Clean milk starts at the teat

Four steps, thirty seconds of patience, and a lower cell count.

Mastitis rarely arrives with a bang. It creeps in through the teat end after milking, when the canal is still open and the barn is full of bacteria.

The fix in the guide is simple and repeatable. Dip with 0.5% iodine or chlorhexidine, wait about 30 seconds, wipe dry, and after milking finish with a barrier dip.

The routine, one tap at a time

Tap each step as you finish it, the way you would at the shed.

Who gets milked first?

Order matters. The guide milks heifers first, then healthy cows, and infected cows last, so one sick cow does not pass bacteria down the line.

Find hidden mastitis before it spreads

Some infections never show in the milk. The guide suggests a California Mastitis Test (CMT) every fortnight. Dry cows get their own protection: dry-cow therapy and bismuth teat sealants.

Safety: Dry-cow antibiotics should only be used on your vet’s advice, and you must respect the milk withdrawal period.
