Why your cows give less milk in summer, and what to do by noon

Heat quietly takes a tenth to a quarter of your milk. Here is how to see it coming.

It is two in the afternoon. Your best cow is standing, breathing fast, not eating. Nothing is wrong with her feed or her health. She is simply too hot.

According to the guide this lesson is built on, once the temperature-humidity index (THI) goes above 72, milk output can fall by 10 to 25%. You will not see a sick cow. You will just see a smaller milk can.

“The cow does not feel the thermometer. She feels heat and humidity together.”

Meet the THI

THI blends air temperature and humidity into one number. A dry 35°C day and a sticky 30°C day can feel equally cruel to a cow, and THI shows why.

What to do when the needle moves

The guide gives three levers. None needs a big investment.

The number that surprises people: lying time

A cow that lies down for 12 to 14 hours a day sends about 25 to 30% more blood through her udder, according to the guide. More blood flow means more milk. Hot, wet or hard stalls steal that rest.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • THI above 72 can cut milk by 10 to 25%.
  • Fans and misters are the first line of defence.
