Is your manure pile healthy? Check it in two minutes

The right mix, the right wetness and the right heat turn smelly waste into something you can use or sell.

Poultry droppings that sit wet and packed in one place go sour. That is where the bad smell comes from, and it brings flies too.

Composting fixes this. Small farms compost their manure. Bigger farms can compost it or make biogas. Either way, it starts with a good pile.

Three numbers to remember

20–25 : 1
The mix. Mix manure with a carbon-rich material such as paddy straw, husk or wood shavings.
35–50%
The wetness. Damp, not dripping. Too wet gets smelly, too dry stops working.
54–66°C
The heat. A hot pile means the good bacteria are working.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • Mix manure with straw or husk at 20 to 25 parts carbon to 1.
  • Keep it 35 to 50% wet and stir it often.
  • A pile at 54 to 66°C is working well.
  • Store it dry, covered, and 2 m above the water table.
